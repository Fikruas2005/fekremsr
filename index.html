Why We Exist

Powerful agronomic science should not beout of reach.

After 20+ years working in research institutions across Africa, Japan, Australia, and the USA — the same frustration repeated everywhere. The tools that could transform agricultural decisions were too expensive, too complex, and completely inaccessible to the people who needed them most. AbayFarm Brain exists to change that — bringing together specialist agronomists, crop modellers, AI engineers, and remote sensing experts to deliver the tools and advice that agriculture deserves.

💰

Too expensive for most users

Professional crop modelling, multispectral drones, and AI advisory systems cost tens of thousands of dollars — inaccessible to most research institutions and entirely out of reach for smallholder farmers.

🔒

Too complex without specialist training

APSIM, DSSAT, and most agronomic AI tools require months of training. Research teams lose time calibrating models that specialists could configure in days.

📵

Unreachable in the field

Farmers in rural Ethiopia, the Africa, Asia, and Europe, and Southeast Asia have a phone and WhatsApp — but no access to the AI advisory that could transform their daily crop decisions.

About AbayFarm Brain
Dr. Fekremariam Asargew Mihretie
Computational Agronomist & Entrepreneur · AI Tools Builder · Founder, AbayFarm Brain

As a computational agronomist with a PhD in Global Dryland Science from Tottori University, Japan, I have spent over 20 years working at the intersection of crop science, soil management, and digital technology — conducting research across Ethiopia, Ireland, Japan, Australia, and the United States.

After years working with powerful crop models, AI systems, and drone remote sensing — I kept watching the same frustration repeat: the technology that could transform agricultural decisions was too expensive, too complex, and completely inaccessible to the farmers who needed it most. I built AbayFarm Brain to change that. CropOracle and PlotScan are free because professional agronomic intelligence should be a right, not a privilege.

Currently a Computational Agronomist and Entrepreneur based in the United States. Previous research roles and international consultancy across Ethiopia, Ireland, Japan, Australia, and the United States — working with leading agricultural research institutions, development organisations, and government programmes. Research published in Field Crops Research, Agricultural Systems, Science of the Total Environment, European Journal of Agronomy, and more.

Crop Modelling · Legume Systems · Australia

Legume-Based Rotation Optimisation — South-Eastern Australian Cropping Systems

Led field experiments and crop model simulation (APSIM Next Gen) on legume-based crop rotations — modelling nitrogen dynamics, soil carbon, profitability, and fertiliser use efficiency across south-eastern Australian cropping systems under multiple climate scenarios.

Outcome
Crop model simulation of nitrogen dynamics and yield outcomes across climate scenarios delivered. Findings provide evidence-based recommendations for commercial grain farmers across SE Australia.

Water Stress · Crop Physiology · Japan · 2022–2023

Photosynthesis–Stomatal Conductance Under Progressive Drought in Rice

Investigated physiological mechanisms of rice response to soil water stress in Japan under controlled conditions. Quantified changes in stomatal conductance–photosynthesis relationship under progressive drought.

Outcome
Published in Science of the Total Environment (2024). Findings improve drought stress simulation in crop models — directly applicable to climate-smart agriculture globally.
📍 Japan📄 Published STE 2024
Smallholder Systems · Ethiopia · ARARI · 2005–2015

Smallholder Crop Management Programme — Ethiopian Highlands

Led a 10-year research programme at ARARI developing and promoting improved soil and nutrient management for Ethiopian highland crops — sorghum, wheat, maize, teff, finger millet. Transitioning smallholders to evidence-based nutrient management.

Outcome
Multiple publications in Field Crops Research, Agronomy Journal. Improved teff management recommendations adopted across the Amhara Region, benefiting thousands of smallholder households.
